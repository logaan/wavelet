goal-6 session 1 finding: this is already RESOLVED IN CODE — recommend closing.

The open question here ("when a compiled macro's body calls the `expand`
builtin, what happens?") was answered by implementation in commit 02fef25
("feat(emit): support `expand` inside a compiled macro body"), which predates
this session. Neither option 1 (reject) nor option 2 (host callback) was taken;
a scoped form of option 3 was:

- emit.rs emits a **guest-internal one-step expander** (`mc_expand_step`) into
  every macro component and routes the `expand` builtin to it (via
  `Emitter.macro_expand_idx`; `None`/error in ordinary non-macro modules).
- It mirrors builtins.rs `expand` exactly: if the argument form is a call
  `(name-MACRO …)` to one of the *library's own* macros, it runs that macro's
  compiled body once over the call's argument forms; otherwise it returns the
  form unchanged.
- The option-3 worry (a frozen expander copy silently disagreeing) is bounded:
  it is a one-step dispatcher over the library's own compiled bodies, and
  agreement with the interpreter is LOCKED by the differential harness —
  `tests/macro_differential.rs::expand_inside_macro_agrees` (`both {x}
  expand(Quasi And Unquote(x) Unquote(x))`), green.
